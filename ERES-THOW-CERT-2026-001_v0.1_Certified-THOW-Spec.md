## Abstract

This specification defines a Certified Self-Sustaining Tiny Home On Wheels (THOW): a mobile dwelling that collects its own water, grows part of its own food by vertical farming, generates its own power, manages its own sanitation, and carries the supplies needed to survive an emergency. It sets one ordering rule above every other: Human Quality of Life Improvement comes first and Human-Performance-Enhancement (HPE) second. The ordering is lexicographic: no gain in performance can justify a loss in quality of life. Evidence is gathered as P³ (Personal, Public, Private) at the User-GROUP level: people hold their own records, only group aggregates of ten or more are published, and private data are never released. Two certification tracks run together: the home (third-party structural and electrical certification plus ERES systems checks) and the builder (a portable trades credential). The builder pathway is written to hold in the worst case, including a person with a violent record whose condition cannot yet be shown to improve: building certified homes is earned standing that repairs harm to self and society, but release is never a production quota or a score, and the housing floor is never withdrawn. Worked figures show that a 204 sq ft roof in a 48-inch rainfall climate yields about 13 gallons a day, enough for a conserving household but not unlimited, and that vertical farming supplements rather than replaces a diet. A standard-library test program checks the specification: 25 tests, 20 pass, 5 skipped by design for the open empirical items. Nothing here has been built or measured in the field; the next step is a prototype, a third-party certification, and a User-GROUP pilot.

> **In plain terms.** A small home on a trailer that catches its own rainwater, grows greens, makes its own power and is stocked for emergencies. The first job is that people living in it have a better life. Making them stronger or more skilled comes second, and can never be done at the cost of the first.

## 1. The ordering principle: Quality of Life first, Performance second

**Rule O1 (lexicographic).** Options are ranked first by their effect on Human Quality of Life (QoL). Human-Performance-Enhancement (HPE) only breaks ties. An HPE intervention is admissible only if it does not lower QoL [PROPOSED; tested].

**Rule O2 (floor before ascent).** QoL rests on an unconditional floor (N): safe shelter, water, food, sanitation, warmth and a way to call for help. HPE belongs to earned ascent (M). M is additive only and may never reduce N [STATED: ENDHOME Survival/Merit Separation; tested].

**Why this order.** HPE in the ERES corpus is a telos, 'to get beyond mediocrity', controlled through REGs, CONTROLs, LOOPs and CAPs under S and $ [STATED]. Pursued first, performance targets can crowd out rest, safety and dignity. Putting QoL first keeps HPE honest: VACATION (NECESSITY in cases) x Paineology + Meritcology becomes a provisioned good, not a reward [STATED; ordering CONSTRUCTED here].

> **In plain terms.** Living well comes before performing well. You can't trade someone's comfort, safety or rest for productivity.

## 2. The home: system specification

| System | Requirement | Target (reference unit) | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structure | Built and certified to a recognised RV or park-model standard by a third party | NFPA 1192 (RV) or ANSI A119.5 (park model); or NOAH tiny-home certification | VALIDATED standards; choice PROPOSED |
| Weight | Full water tank, dry weight and load within trailer GVWR | Check every build; 200 gal tank = 1,668 lb | DERIVED; tested |
| Water: catch | Roof rainwater harvesting with first-flush diverter | 204 sq ft roof, 48 in/yr, 80% capture: about 4,880 gal/yr, 13.4 gal/day | DERIVED; tested |
| Water: treat | Sediment filter, carbon, and UV or equivalent; potable output tested | Meets EPA drinking-water standards at certification and annually | VALIDATED standard; schedule PROPOSED |
| Water: store | Potable storage plus emergency reserve | 100-200 gal tank; 14-day reserve at 1 gal/person/day | PROPOSED |
| Food | Vertical hydroponic or aeroponic grow system | Greens and herbs year-round; supplements diet, does not replace calories | PROPOSED; calorie limit DERIVED |
| Power | Rooftop solar with battery storage | 1-3 kW array; 5-15 kWh battery; logged uptime | PROPOSED |
| Sanitation | Composting toilet; greywater handled per local code | No untreated discharge | PROPOSED; code VALIDATED |
| Safety | Smoke and CO alarms, extinguisher, two exits | Tested monthly | VALIDATED practice |
| Emergency | 72-hour to 14-day kit built in (GEAR/DERR/ERES list) | Trunk-kit contents scaled to household | PROPOSED |
| Comms | Radio, phone power, HFVN-ready access to the NBERS call | Works off-grid | PROPOSED; HFVN voice read CONJECTURE |

**Water, worked honestly.** One inch of rain on one square foot is 144 cubic inches, or 0.623 gallons [VALIDATED]. An 8.5 x 24 ft roof is 204 sq ft. At about 48 inches a year (typical of Northwest Arkansas; confirm against local climate normals) and 80% capture, that is about 4,880 gallons a year, or 13.4 a day [DERIVED; tested]. That covers drinking and cooking with a wide margin and supports a conserving one- or two-person household. It does not support conventional water use, and rain is seasonal, so storage and conservation are part of the design. Storage is limited by weight: 200 gallons adds 1,668 lb [DERIVED].

**Food, worked honestly.** A vertical grow system in a tiny home can supply fresh greens and herbs year-round. It cannot supply a full diet's calories; staples come from storage, the community cluster or purchase. The certification checks that the system runs and is logged, not that the household is food self-sufficient [PROPOSED].

> **In plain terms.** The roof catches about 13 gallons a day here. That's plenty for drinking and careful living, not for long showers. The grow wall gives you fresh vegetables, not all your meals.

## 3. Certification: two tracks

### 3.1 Home certification

- **Third party:** structure and electrical to NFPA 1192, ANSI A119.5 or NOAH, by an independent inspector [VALIDATED standards].
- **ERES systems check** [PROPOSED]: potable water test passed; 30 days of water-yield and power-uptime logs; sanitation working; grow system producing; emergency kit complete and dated; alarms tested.
- **All checks required.** One failure withholds certification until fixed (tested). Remediation, not rejection: the unit is repaired and re-checked.

### 3.2 Builder certification (TE(A)CH_AG: Hue-Man_CERT)

- **Seven modules** [PROPOSED]: carpentry and framing; electrical (low-voltage and solar); plumbing and water treatment; solar and storage; vertical growing; safety (OSHA-10 or equivalent); and project completion of one certified unit.
- **Portable credential.** Modules map to recognised trade credentials where they exist (for example NCCER and OSHA-10) so the skill counts outside the program [VALIDATED credentials; mapping PROPOSED].
- **SCALULAR pillars.** The modules serve the four capacities Health, Law, Protection, and Skills and Trades [STATED].

## 4. The builder pathway, including the worst case

The pathway is designed against its hardest case: a person with a violent record whose RESONANCE is poor and cannot yet be tuned [STATED by author]. Building certified homes lets that person improve their own condition and repair harm to society in a measurable way. The home and the skill can be certified even when the person's inner state cannot [DERIVED].

| Guardrail | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| G1 No quota release | Release is decided by courts and parole on present condition. Units built are not an input (tested). | Otherwise prison labor becomes the supply engine: the failure mode the corpus already rejected. |
| G2 No score gate | An unmeasured RESONANCE score cannot gate release (tested). | If it can't be tuned, it can't be trusted to decide liberty. |
| G3 Floor holds | The N-floor is never withdrawn, including for this person (tested). | 100% of people means everyone, or the floor is a privilege. |
| G4 Voluntary and paid | Participation is chosen; work is paid at a fair rate; credentials belong to the builder. | Repair that is coerced is not repair. |
| G5 Safety stays with the law | The program does not override public-safety decisions or supervision conditions. | Rehabilitation and protection are separate duties. |
| G6 Non-punitive failure | A mistake leads to isolate, downgrade, retrain, re-check (Paineology), not expulsion. | Remediation produces skill; expulsion produces nothing. |

The worst-case person is one inflow among many. Others include people leaving hospital, displaced by disaster, leaving foster care, leaving the military or fleeing violence [STATED: ENDHOME]. The floor is defined by present condition, not by history.

> **In plain terms.** Even someone who has done serious harm can learn to build homes that shelter others, and earn real credentials doing it. That work counts in their favour. But nobody buys their freedom with houses, and nobody loses their own shelter as punishment.

## 5. Empirics: P³ at the User-GROUP level

Evidence is collected at the level of the User-GROUP (the community bound by its service agreement), on three faces [STATED: P³]:

| Face | What it holds | Who sees it |
|---|---|---|
| Personal | The resident's own QoL survey, health notes, skills record | The resident; shared only by their choice |
| Public | Group aggregates: QoL mean, water yield, uptime, certifications, credentials earned | Everyone, but only for groups of 10 or more (tested) |
| Private | Identity, legal record, individual health data | Never published; ratable, not released (VEILED) |

### 5.1 Metrics, in priority order

- **Primary: Quality of Life.** WHOQOL-BREF (WHO's 26-item instrument: physical, psychological, social, environment) [VALIDATED instrument]; housing stability; household food security (USDA module) [VALIDATED]; water security days; safety incidents.
- **Secondary: Human-Performance-Enhancement.** Credentials earned; functional fitness and health measures chosen by the resident; employment and income after the program. HPE results are reported only alongside QoL, never alone.
- **Systems.** Water yield and quality, power uptime, grow output, emergency-kit readiness.

### 5.2 Pre-registered tests

- **E1.** QoL improves at the User-GROUP level against a comparison group. If it does not, the program is revised before any HPE claim is made.
- **E2.** A built unit's measured water yield over 12 months falls within 25% of the derived figure. If not, the water model is withdrawn.
- **E3.** Grow output and household food security improve. If not, the food claim is reduced.
- **E4.** Builders complete credentials and reach stable housing and work. If not, the pathway is redesigned.
- **E5.** A prototype passes third-party certification.

## 7. HowWay applied

- **GEAR (record):** yield, uptime, water tests, kit dates and certifications are logged as they happen.
- **DERR (diagnose):** low yield, failed water test, battery decline or an expired kit item raises a flag.
- **ERES (remediate):** repair, re-test, re-certify. The repair is logged back into GEAR.
- **Six questions:** the home can show that it is working, show its telemetry, collect its own evidence, detect change, escalate a failure, and do so inside daily life rather than at an annual inspection.

## 8. Test program results
