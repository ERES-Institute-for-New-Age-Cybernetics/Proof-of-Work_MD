# Bug-Out Kit for Emergency Management

Oct 9, 2026 · @Joseph Allen Sprute

## Introduction

A complete bug-out kit for two people weighs about **450 lb**, fills about **38 cubic feet packed**, and costs about **$3,881**. It fits in a midsize SUV with the rear seats up, or a pickup bed.

**Context.** A bug-out kit supports a household that must leave home quickly during an emergency: wildfire, flood, severe storm, ice event, infrastructure failure, or civil disruption. In emergency management terms, it covers the gap between evacuation and reaching shelter, family, or restored services. The kit must be pre-packed, because evacuation orders often allow minutes, not hours.

**Purpose.** This paper lists every item in the kit, estimates its cost, weight and volume, and states what vehicle space is needed to move it in one trip.

**Planning assumptions.**

- 2 adults, self-sufficient for 72 hours (the standard FEMA planning window), with gear that extends further
- Evacuation by vehicle; the full kit is too heavy to carry on foot
- Cold-weather capable (insulated coveralls, boots, firewood)
- Prices are mid-range US retail estimates, not live quotes
- Weights and volumes are typical for the item class, packed in bins or bags

## Transport Requirements

The kit needs about **38 cubic feet** of cargo space and adds about **450 lb** of payload. Items take up about 31 cubic feet on their own; bins, bags and gaps add roughly 25%.

| Vehicle | Typical cargo space (ft³) | Fits the full kit? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pickup, 6.5' bed | 60+ | Yes, with room to spare; needs a cover or tarp |
| Full-size SUV, seats up | 40–50 | Yes |
| Midsize SUV, seats up | 35–40 | Yes, just; little room left |
| Compact SUV, seats up | 25–30 | No; fold one rear seat or drop firewood and plastic sheeting |
| Sedan trunk | 13–17 | No; backpacks and essentials only |

**Payload.** Kit (450 lb) plus two adults (about 350 lb) is roughly 800 lb. Most SUVs and pickups are rated 1,000–1,500 lb; check the sticker on the driver's door jamb.

**Loading.**

- Heaviest items low and forward: water (50 lb), tow chain (22 lb), jack, firewood
- Fuel can outside the cabin: hitch cargo carrier or truck bed, upright and strapped
- Rifle and handgun in the locked case, kept separate from ammunition where state law requires
- The two backpacks loaded last, on top, ready to grab

**If the vehicle must be abandoned.** The two backpacks become the kit. At about 30–35 lb each, they carry water and purifier, food, first aid and trauma kit, firestarters, a flashlight, the radio, rain gear, space blankets, documents and cash, a knife and the multi-tool. Everything else stays.

**Ways to reduce weight and cost.**

- Skip firewood and gather on site: −60 lb, −3 ft³
- Drop the 24' × 100' plastic sheeting if the tarp suffices: −46 lb
- Choose a lightweight tarp over heavy-duty: about −15 lb
- Subtract anything already owned; tools, clothing and bedding are often on hand
- The solar station ($300) and firearms ($1,100) are the largest single costs
